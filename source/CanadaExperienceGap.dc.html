3What we bet onDesign
Primary hypothesis

A bottom sheet with explicit current and new limit, stronger language and an intentional confirmation will cut accidental decreases and related contacts.

Secondary hypothesis

Competitors train people to think in increments, and warnings fail because people skim low-risk financial flows.

Reframed the issue as a mental-model problem, as measured by four approaches weighed against call, survey and competitor evidence, by treating skimming as the cause instead of rewording the warning

06 · Takeaways

Four rules from one misread limit.

Rule 01

People skim, so show the math

What I saw
A warning went unread in a low-risk money flow.
Next time
Put the consequence in numbers, where people type.
Rule 02

Design the pattern, not the patch

What I saw
Canada surfaced it. Any market with both paths has the same trap.
Next time
Ask which other markets share the trap.
Rule 03

Let scope follow impact

What I saw
Only 0.2% were affected, so we fixed the flow in place.
Next time
Size the problem before sizing the solution.
Rule 04

Constraints are the brief

What I saw
Decrease had to stay possible. Increase stayed primary.
Next time
Write the constraints down first.

2The harder questionProduct · Design
Fraud, scam, social engineering“Was this you?” has three honest answers. Only one is simple.
Fraud

Someone else used the card. The Card Member did not take part.

They answerNo
Scam

The Card Member was tricked into paying, by a false story.

They answerYes, but tricked
Social engineering

A caller or message steered the choice, step by step.

They answerYes, but manipulated

So version one makes the “no” path calm and quick. It also lays the ground to spot risk behind a “yes” later.

2What we believedDesign-level assumptions, not measured
What I did
Wrote five beliefs about people in a scare, before any screens.
Why
So each design choice had a belief to be checked against, not a taste.
What it gave me
Three rules: reassure first, show recognition cues, ask one thing at a time.
If it exists todayA spinner, or a blank white screenNo hint of what is happening during the checks.
The inner voice“Did the app crash?”“Is my card being locked?”“Why is it taking so long?”
Trust and anxietyThey assume the worst first.
Cognitive loadThey scan. They don’t read.
ControlControl, not responsibility.
SpeedSlower feels riskier.
Mental model“It’s me” or “It’s fraud”.
3The user flowDesign
What I did
Mapped every way in and every path out, for each answer.
Why
To see every dead end before drawing a screen.
What it gave me
Four entries share one review screen. A not-recognized charge gets its own fraud path.
Entry pointDecisionScreen or outcomeFraud path
4Mapping the journeyPain points · emotions · opportunities
What I did
Walked seven stages from the alert to closure. Noted emotion, pain and opportunity at each.
Why
To find where anxiety peaks, so reassurance lands there.
What it gave me
The peaks are the decision and the “not mine” moment. Both got crisis-mode copy and a clear next step.
5Making sense of itSynthesis
What I did
Sorted every note into five buckets: content, UX, UI, trust and interaction.
Why
To turn scattered notes into actions someone could build.
What it gave me
One to three design actions per bucket. These became the brief for the five concepts.
Observe

A scared person scans. Names alone don’t ring a bell.

Learn

Reassurance, recognition and a clear next step matter most.

Make

Reassure first. Show cues. Ask one thing per charge.

08 · Takeaways

Four rules for designing in a scare.

Rule 01

Design for panic, not a normal task

What I saw
Scared people scan. They do not read.
Next time
Lead with reassurance. Ask one thing.
Rule 02

Help recognition, not recall

What I saw
A merchant name alone did not ring a bell.
Next time
Add logo, time, place and category.
Rule 03

Calm authority beats alarm

What I saw
Red-heavy and gamified ideas felt wrong for money.
Next time
State first, color second.
Rule 04

Close the loop

What I saw
Relief fades without closure.
Next time
End with what we did and how to stay safe.

Design rules I took from this

These are my inferences from the data, not research findings.

Rule 1Share a link, not an appWhatsApp is the front door. A card should open in one tap.
Rule 2Write in the family’s languageSix languages in the studio, with mixed wording allowed.
Rule 3One action per screenUnfinished parts say Soon. Nothing is over-promised.
Rule 4Parents decide, grandparents receiveDesign the logged-in tools for the fifty-somethings. Give elders a link.
4Money

Half the budget sits with vendors a family must find.

Venue, catering, decor and photography add up to 45–60% of a typical budget (my sum of the ranges). Jewellery and apparel are retail, with their own channels.

Each of these is a different vendor to find, compare and book. That is where trust and availability decide the choice.

Jewellery and apparel are shopped in person and through established retailers. Not where a family-run platform starts.

5The invitation

The card is the smallest saving. And the best door.

Move the sliders. Printing is real money, but against a wedding budget it is tiny. What the card holds is worth more: the date, the city, the language and the guest list.

Business lens

Three hypotheses to test. None is validated, and no revenue model is decided.

Hypothesis 1
Cards travel

Each card reaches about 330 guests, so every family acts as a free channel.

Test: cards shared per event, and opens per card.
Hypothesis 2
Intent is the asset

A family with a date, city and ceremony list is a qualified buyer for the 45–60% of budget that vendors fight over.

Test: vendor enquiries per event.
Hypothesis 3
Vendors pay for fit

Referrals lead and only 1 in 3 couples read reviews, so a family’s trusted shortlist is worth more than a ranking.

Test: repeat bookings from shared shortlists.
Who is already here
Wedding platforms center on couple and vendor

WedMeGood and WeddingWire India are established. WedMeGood has launched an AI chatbot, Wedika, and says 24% of its vendors use AI tools.

ShubhViva’s bet
Center on the whole family, from the card

Start in the family’s language, with the invitation, then grow into planning and vendors. This is my positioning, not a market finding.

04 · Takeaways

Four rules from building for families.

Rule 01

Start where it’s visible

What I saw
The invitation is the first thing a family shares.
Next time
Begin with the output people show others.
Rule 02

Language is the product

What I saw
Six scripts made the card feel like theirs.
Next time
Localize the content, not just the labels.
Rule 03

Be honest about what’s live

What I saw
Only the studio is live, and the app says what is coming.
Next time
Mark Soon, so nothing is over-promised.
Rule 04

Keep the decisions

What I saw
AI helped build it. The scope and the calls were mine.
Next time
Use AI for speed, not for judgment.

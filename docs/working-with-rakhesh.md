# Working with Rakhesh: standing preferences

These are Rakhesh's own stated preferences. They apply to every change in this repo and to the résumé.

## How he wants answers
- Crisp, short answers. If he says "I didn't understand", restate in fewer, plainer words.
- Tell him the plan before designing when he asks ("let me know points before designing").
- Never push live unless he says "push live" or "push". Publishing to the canvas is always fine.
- Answer comment threads on the canvas with a reply in the thread.

## Design taste
- Solid fills for chart bars, never gradients.
- No more than two text styles in one line of copy (regular + one accent).
- Dislikes strokes and outlines on UI shapes: use soft fills and soft shadows. (A hairline card border from the design system is the only accepted line. A vertical divider is fine when he asks for one.)
- Never drift from the design-system language for reusable components (fonts, prev/next, footer).
- Everything scannable: short, crisp copy.

## Portfolio content rules
- Impact statements use Google's X-Y-Z formula: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]".
- Case studies follow a scene flow: the ask, my push-back, leaders' decision, test or outcome, takeaways. Use "Takeaways" or "Learnings", never "Reflect". Use flags for markets.
- Do not use internal team names or acronyms in case studies. Use plain descriptive wording.
- When a first-person quote says "he", use Rakhesh's name where needed so the credit is clear.
- Conversation scenes: real unDraw illustrations recoloured to the design language (not hand-drawn figures); Business/Product on the left, Design (Rakhesh) on the right; callout speech bubbles; no background container; micro-interactions on charts.
- Research sections (hypotheses, user flow, journey map, synthesis): crisp rows of what I did / why / what it gave, with small clean whiteboard-style visuals (regenerated, not camera photos). Diagrams fill the width and can be pinch-zoomed instead of scrolling sideways. Applies to all cases (only Digital Charge Verification has all four steps so far).
- Never invent a metric. Targets, estimates and concepts are labelled as such. Never turn a target into an achieved result.
